How Do I Read My Profile?
The AgileBrain framework maps human emotion into four life domains – the self, material, social and spiritual domains – and at three levels – from foundational, to experiential, to aspirational - resulting in a total of twelve categories of emotional need. The AgileBrain exercise exposes images for short periods – just enough time to respond emotionally, not rationally. The images represent both the positive (hope) and negative (fear) aspects of these twelve emotional needs categories.
Each cell is color-coded to reflect the intensity of your emotional response: darker shades indicate stronger emotional responses, suggesting more unmet need in that area; lighter shades indicate lighter emotional responses, suggesting less unmet need in that area. Another way to think about a light shade is that you are more "settled" in that emotional need category. For your strongest emotional responses, you will also see a color-coded circle showing the relative contributions of positive and negative emotional needs: more blue in the circle indicates that positive needs contributed more than negative ones; more orange in the circle indicates negative needs contributed more than positive ones. Equal blue and orange indicate that your positive and negative needs contributed equally in that need category. A solid circle indicates all your selections were either positive (blue) or negative (orange).

Why do the images disappear so quickly?
The most common reaction people new to AgileBrain express is a desire for more time to select the images. We know from neuroscience that the brain processes images in a specific way. The brain takes about 200 milliseconds to recognize an image. Then, the brain responds emotionally to the image. After about a second the brain begins to rationalize what it is seeing. AgileBrain measures emotional reaction and filters out the rational response. If you had more time to react to the image, your rational response would start to take over. As they say, "go with your gut."
How Does AgileBrain Work?
The AgileBrain methodology plumbs the mind’s depths systematically and with high validity. The AgileBrain image-based exercise provides a sentence completion prompt that you complete by selecting from a set of related, validated images. AgileBrain maps your responses (both the images selected and the speed of those selections) corresponding to each of the twelve emotional cells (four domains – self, material, social, spiritual – and 3 levels – foundational, experiential, aspirational). An algorithm is then applied that converts the selection data into a measure of emotional intensity. When used in combination with traditional assessment measures, AgileBrain has been shown to signiﬁcantly increase the explanatory power of the outcome you are trying to measure.

What's with the Iceberg in the AgileBrain Logo?
Icebergs ﬂoat low with only 10% showing above the waterline. Our brains are the same. Neuroscience shows that only 10% of brain activity occurs at the conscious (cognitive or rational) level. That means fully 90% of brain activity, including your emotions, operate “below the waterline.” If you think of the brain as an iceberg, then using a traditional cognitive assessment only scratches the surface. But we know that there’s a lot lurking under the surface! That’s what AgileBrain is all about.

How Can I Be Sure that AgileBrain Works?
The AgileBrain methodology has been developed by behavioral scientists over a twenty-year period. The approach has been independently validated and shown to provide more accurate measurement than other so-called neuroscience-based assessments. Ultimately, however, the most important test is you. Did the AgileBrain exercise and the feedback you’ve received resonate with you? If so, we’re glad and encourage you to reﬂect on and take positive steps to address your emotional needs.

I Thought Anger, Joy, and Envy Were Emotions?
They are but they are “presenting” emotions. AgileBrain measures the underlying emotional needs that can present as anger (e.g., feeling that you have been treated unjustly), joy (e.g.,experiencing success), envy (e.g., when a coworker is recognized but you remain unrecognized) or other presenting feelings. If you experience a presenting feeling, use the AgileBrain framework to help ask what’s going on deeper.

Can I Take AgileBrain Again?
Yes, we would be delighted to see you again. AgileBrain is designed to measure your emotional / motivational state at a speciﬁc time. So, don’t retake it right now, but do come back when you feel diﬀerently (stuck/unstuck, growing/stagnating, encouraged/discouraged) or when your situation changes (e.g., new job, promotion, layoff, new family situation, new life event).

Who is Leading Indicator Systems?
Leading Indicator Systems (or LIS for short) is a Boston-based (USA) ﬁrm dedicated to human capital research and assessment. LIS employees are software engineers, program designers, and social scientists. The AgileBrain framework and assessment are innovations from LIS.
